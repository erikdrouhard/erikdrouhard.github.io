NUANCE · 2021–2023 · PUBLICLY: VERSE

Assembling a Mix interface out of requirements

A design system is not a theme. This prototype tests whether a visitor can answer five product requirements, watch Verse translate them down through tokens, components, and layout, and arrive at a finished Mix surface that could not have been anything else.

  • DESIGN SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
  • FRONT-END ARCHITECTURE

01 · THE QUESTION UNDER TEST

Should the visitor choose requirements, or preferences?

The easy version of this page is a configurator: pick a colour, pick a radius, watch a button change. It reads as a theme generator, and it explains nothing about why anyone built a design system.

The version below asks a visitor to answer the questions a Mix feature team actually had to answer — what is the author doing, where does it live, how much is on screen, what state are they in, who is driving — and then shows Verse deriving the interface from those answers. The button variant is an output, never an input.

If the derivation is legible, the visitor learns what a design system is for. If it is not, this is an expensive Storybook and the branching model is wrong.

02 · THE SYSTEM LADDER

Four layers, in the system's own vocabulary

Verse was structured so a team could take one layer and leave the rest — tokens without components, components without layouts. These are the system's real names, kept as they were rather than translated into generic atomic-design terms.

  • 01

    Verse Tokens

    Colour, space, radius, type, focus, and target size as named values. Composable as utility classes, platform agnostic, and a shared vocabulary between design and front-end.

  • 02

    Core Components

    Button, Input, Textbox, Select, Label, Text, Popover. Each one owns its interactive and accessible states — the states static design files kept leaving out.

  • 03

    Layout Components

    HStack and VStack. One composition model shared by Figma auto layout and the React implementation, so a design and its code described the same structure.

  • 04

    Mix Pages

    The product surfaces built on top. Mix.dialog and the surrounding platform consumed the layers above rather than restating them.

03 · THE DESCENT

State the problem. Then follow it down the ladder.

This is a walk, not a control panel. State five requirements, then descend one rung at a time — Verse Tokens, Core Components, Layout Components, and finally a Mix page. Each rung is built only out of the rung above it, and every chapter you finish stays on the page so the whole derivation is readable at the end.

Change a requirement at any point and the story rewinds to the rung where that answer first mattered, because everything below it was derived from the answer you just replaced.

04 · WHAT THIS PROTOTYPE IS ASKING YOU

Does the branching model hold?

Five open items. The prototype exists to make these answerable by reacting rather than by arguing.

  • Requirements over preferences

    Does answering “what is the author doing?” feel more revealing than picking a variant — or does it feel like a quiz before the payoff?

  • Five dimensions, or fewer

    Task, context, density, state, and input method are all here. Two or three may carry the argument on their own, and the rest may be noise.

  • Three outcomes

    Interaction context produces the inline, panel, and overlay endings. Are those distinct enough to feel like arriving somewhere?

  • The conflict case

    Inline plus data-dense deliberately does not fit, and the system says so instead of reflowing quietly. Is that the most convincing moment here, or a distraction?

  • Layer navigation

    The four layers are a rail you move between. A single scroll that descends the ladder is the obvious alternative.

  • Where the buttons live

    The variant-by-state matrix sits inside Core Components. It could instead be the finale, since states were the thing static design kept dropping.

PROVENANCE

What is original, and what is reconstructed

  • Original. The layer names, the HStack / VStack composition model, the Button.Primary and Popover component syntax, the token-as-utility-class approach, and the accessibility motivation all come from the system and from Erik's i3 2022 talk, “Design like a developer… Develop like a designer!”
  • Reconstructed. Every pixel in the specimen. The token values, the field content, the Mix page around it, and the derivation rules were written for this prototype to demonstrate the model. None of it is a production screenshot.
  • Attribution. Verse was a shared initiative. Erik led its design and front-end architecture — components, patterns, tokens, implementation practices, and adoption guidance — working as the design–engineering liaison across multiple engineering teams and a second design group. He did not build it alone.
  • Chronology. Verse is the public name. Bolt was the earlier name, which is why the source slides say Bolt. Verse is a later chapter in the broader Mix.dialog and platform arc, and was migrated to Fluent 2 after the Microsoft acquisition.
